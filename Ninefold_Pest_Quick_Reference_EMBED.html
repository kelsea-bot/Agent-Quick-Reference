Call flow10 steps
1
Answer & greet — "Thank you for calling Ninefold Pest, this is [your name]"
2
Verify account — get their name and address before anything else
3
Identify reason for call — let them explain fully before responding
4
Acknowledge & empathize — don't skip this, even on quick calls
5
Pull up account & review notes
6
Ask diagnostic questions — pest type, location in home, when first seen
7
Offer solution / next steps — schedule, educate, or transfer as needed
8
Confirm details back to customer before closing
9
Add notes to account — what was said, what was resolved
10
Close warmly — recap, ask if there's anything else, thank them by name

Transfers & escalationsWho gets what
New customer cancel — no initial service yet
Follow initial service cancel protocol. Let the customer know their sales agent will reach out to follow up and confirm everything. If they have an upcoming appointment, confirm it will not be serviced. Send all info to April's chat — she routes to RC/sales. Do not transfer the call to April.
Established customer cancel → Account Manager (Retention)
Any customer who has already had their initial service and wants to cancel or downgrade — transfer to Account Manager.
Termites → Lead Generators queue (TalkDesk)
We do not handle termite services. Transfer immediately — do not attempt to schedule, quote, or advise on termites.
Same-day anything → April's chat
Same-day service changes, tech no-shows, same-day ETAs or add-ons — message April before committing to anything with the customer.
Supervisor callback — last resort only
Only when you have genuinely tried to de-escalate and cannot resolve the issue. Offer a callback within 24–48 hours — do not promise an immediate call.
Always attempt to resolve first. This is not a default — it is a last option.
Scheduling rulesRoute tiers
Route is open — schedule it
Tech is in the area, not overbooked, and the stop is close to the existing route. No approval needed — go ahead and book.
5–9 miles from route — Team Lead or Supervisor approval required
Check distance in PestRoutes. If the stop falls 5–9 miles out, get approval from Team Lead or Supervisor before confirming with the customer.
Offer a callback: "Let me make sure we can fit you in — I'll give you a call back as soon as I've confirmed everything on our end."
10+ miles from route — Team Lead or Supervisor first, then Clarissa's sheet
Get Team Lead or Supervisor approval first, then add to Clarissa's sheet. Clarissa responds on her responses sheet to approve or deny. Do not add to her sheet without going through Team Lead or Supervisor first.
Do not confirm with the customer until Clarissa's response comes through.
Reminder day — next 2 calendar days need approval
Reminders go out 2 days before an appointment. If today is 5/5, reminders for 5/7 are going out today — meaning any appointment on 5/6 or 5/7 requires Team Lead or Supervisor approval before adding to the route. Anything for today (5/5) is a same-day request and goes to April's chat.

ScriptsReady-to-use phrases
Opening
"Thank you for calling Ninefold Pest, this is [your name] — can I get your name and the address on your account?"
Scheduling callback (approval needed)
"I want to make sure we get you taken care of — let me confirm the best fit on our end and I'll give you a call right back to lock that in."
New customer cancel
"I completely understand. I want to make sure everything is handled correctly for you — your sales agent will be reaching out to follow up and confirm everything. If you have an upcoming appointment, please know that it will not be serviced in the meantime."
Transferring to Account Manager
"I completely understand — let me get you over to one of our account managers who handles that directly. One moment."
Supervisor callback (last resort)
"I hear you, and I want to make sure this gets the attention it deserves. Let me have a supervisor reach out to you within 24 to 48 hours — can I confirm the best number to call you back on?"
Closing
"Is there anything else I can help you with today, [name]? Great — thank you so much for calling Ninefold Pest, have a wonderful day!"
Dead air & hold procedureKey reminders
RuleDead air limit is 10 seconds — if you need a moment, say something. Don't go silent on the customer
RuleAlways ask permission before placing on hold — never put someone on hold without warning
RuleCheck back in before 1 minute on hold — every time, no exceptions
RuleIf you're typing or looking something up, use a bridge phrase so the customer knows you're still there
Placing on hold
"Can I place you on a brief hold while I look into that for you? I'll be right back."
Bridge phrase while working
"I'm pulling that up for you right now, just one moment."
Check-in on hold
"Thank you so much for holding — I'm still working on this for you, I'll have an answer shortly."

Language guideDon't say this → say this instead
✕"Contract"
→"Service agreement" or "plan"
✕"I need to get approval from my supervisor"
→"I need to confirm the routing on our end — I'll call you right back"
✕"I need to get approval from the tech"
→"Let me make a couple of adjustments and confirm that date — I'll follow up shortly"
✕Jumping to "we don't service that" with no questions
→Ask clarifying questions first, identify a related service if possible, then redirect
✕Over-apologizing — "I'm sorry, I'm sorry"
→Acknowledge once, then move to solving the issue
✕Offering a credit or discount immediately
→Exhaust other options first — credits are a last resort, not a first offer
